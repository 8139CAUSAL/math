The Illusion Evaporated

Group A's “superiority” vanished. On re-test, their scores regressed toward the average. They weren't special—they just had a lucky day.

This is regression to the mean. When you select a group because they scored high once, they will almost always score lower the next time—not because anything changed, but because extreme results are rarely repeated.

The Lesson
A p-value < 0.05 doesn't mean you found truth. It means you found something unusual enough to be worth investigating. If you test 20 hypotheses, one will be “significant” by pure chance. This is P-hacking: running enough tests until the noise aligns in your favor, then publishing only that result.
